Artifact 4: ML Data Readiness Checklist

AIML-500 Fundamentals of AI and ML · Workshop 4 · November 2026

Introduction

In Workshop 4 I worked through three data challenges with an AI coach: cleaning a messy review dataset, scaling a résumé-screening model to a new country, and two hospitals sharing patient data. The same lesson kept coming up. Most machine learning problems start in the data, not the model. I turned my answers into a two-page checklist that a team can actually use.

Description

Page 1 is the checklist itself, with four sections and an "evidence to look for" column so a box only gets ticked when there's proof. Page 2 shows the four checkpoints as a flow, three worked examples from the scenarios, red flags that mean "stop and check," and a sign-off box.

SectionWhat it checks
1. Before you change anythingRaw copy kept, data profiled, rules for "unusable" written down
2. Cleaning and labelsDuplicates removed before splitting, labels checked, no groups dropped by accident
3. Bias and scalingWhat the label really measures, proxies, error rates by group, local pilot, appeals
4. Sharing sensitive dataFederated learning and its limits, governance agreement, validation at each site

Download the checklist (PDF)

Objective

I wanted to show that I can spot data risks before they become model problems, and turn that into something practical. A checklist is easier to follow during a busy project than a long write-up, especially for teams building healthcare tools, where a missed case affects a real patient.

Process

  • Worked through the three scenarios with the AI coach, giving a decision and a reason for each and answering follow-up questions.
  • Pulled out the checks that came up in my answers and the coach's feedback, such as removing duplicates before splitting, looking for proxy variables, and validating at each hospital separately.
  • Grouped them by stage, from first look at the data to sharing it with a partner.
  • Cut each item down to one line and added a short "why" under each section so the reason is clear.
  • Added an evidence column, the checkpoint flow, worked examples, red flags, and a sign-off box so it works as a real project tool, kept in the same black-and-white style as the rest of my portfolio.

Tools and Technologies Used

  • The course's AI coaching prompt, run in Claude (Anthropic)
  • Claude to help organize the checklist and lay out the PDF
  • HTML and CSS for the design, exported to PDF

Value Proposition

Unique value: It turns three realistic data problems into two pages of checks that a team can tick off before training, scaling, or sharing a model.

Relevance: In healthcare software, a model that looks accurate overall can still fail one hospital or one group of patients. This checklist shows I think about data quality, fairness, and privacy as part of building the system, not as an afterthought.
